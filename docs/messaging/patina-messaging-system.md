Supersedes the voice guidance previously scattered across `Design/patina-implementation-guide.md`, `patina_site_modification_spec.md` §13, and `Design/Patina_Marketing_Summary_v2.md`.*

## 0. How to use this document

Before writing any customer-facing words — a headline, a button, an error message, an email — check three things here:
1. **Positioning** (§1): Are we saying the one true thing Patina is?
2. **Track** (§2): Who is this for — trade or consumer? Am I using that track's pillars and CTA?
3. **Voice & lexicon** (§3–4): Does it sound like us? Does it use a "say" word, not a "don't" word?

If a claim can't be backed by §6 (Proof), cut it. If it promises something that doesn't exist yet (§7 Honesty rules), cut it or date it.

## 1. Positioning

### The one line (master statement)
> **Patina puts a great designer's eye within reach — a designer-taught way to find furniture that belongs in your space, with a full designer engagement ready whenever the time is right.**

Everything ladders up to this. Two halves matter equally: *(a)* a great designer's eye, made accessible — the judgment is the prize, and Patina is how you get it; *(b)* a path from self-serve to a full designer engagement when you want one. That second half — **the handoff** — is Patina's most defensible idea. Lead with it more than we do today.

### Tagline (unchanged, protected)
> **Where Time Adds Value.**

This is the brand's emotional core and its name's meaning. Use it as the signature, not as an explainer. Never dilute it with a second competing tagline.

### The name, as a belief
> *Patina (n.): the surface that develops through age and exposure — adding depth, character, and value.*

The opposite of disposable. This is why the brand exists: furniture (and taste, and a company) that grows **more** valuable with time. Use the etymology when we need the "why," not on every page.

### Descriptor variants (pick by space)
- **Search/meta (≤155 char):** *Designer-taught furniture discovery — find pieces that belong in your space, then hand off to a designer who can take it further. Where time adds value.*
- **One sentence (press/bios):** *Patina is a designer-taught furniture platform from Madison, Wisconsin, where working interior designers — not an algorithm chasing clicks — decide what belongs in your space.*
- **Elevator (spoken):** *"You know how a great designer just knows what works in a room? Patina puts that eye within reach for everyone — and when you're ready for the real thing, a designer picks up exactly where you left off."*

### What we are NOT (kills the ambiguity)
- Not a mega-catalog. We show a **focused** selection, not thousands of SKUs.
- Not an "AI furniture app." AI is the mechanism, designers are the authority (§5).
- Not "curated" theater. Every maker earned placement through craft — *no sponsored slots, no pay-to-play.*
- Not available-now. The app and marketplace are **pre-launch**; we say so plainly.

## 2. Audience — dual-track, shared voice

Patina is multi-sided. We run **two messaging spines with one voice**. Every page belongs primarily to one track and states which CTA it drives.

### Shared spine (true for everyone)
Craft over volume · Designer-taught, not algorithm-fed · Madison/Midwest honesty · Time adds value · Built in public.

### Track A — Trade (interior designers, architects, high-end trade)
*The audience this brand review prioritizes, and the product (Designer Portal) that's actually live.*

- **Who:** Working interior designers (solo + small firms), and architects specifying FF&E / millwork on residential and boutique-commercial projects. People with taste, opinions, and clients — who source for a living.
- **What they want to hear:** *You are the intelligence, not the user.* Co-creation over "beta access." Practice efficiency (the briefing package that kills the 90-minute intake). A lead pipeline they don't have today. Ownership of the taxonomy. Named credit.
- **Message pillars:**
  1. **Built by a designer, for designers.** Leah runs a real studio; her taste teaches the system.
  2. **Co-create, don't test.** The Founding 50 shape the taxonomy and own the narrative.
  3. **A running start on every client.** The app hands you a briefing package — style profile, room scans, saves, budget — before the first meeting.
  4. **Your expertise, credited and compensated in leads.** First designers consumers can connect with at launch.
- **Architect note (light touch — no dedicated persona yet):** speak to *specification, project scale (single room → whole home → multi-unit), and coordination*, not just "sourcing." Recommend a future `/for-architects` surface or a fold-in section on `/designers`; do not invent architect testimonials or capabilities we don't have.
- **Surfaces:** `/designers` (Founding 50), `/services` (their prospective clients), Designer Portal.
- **Primary CTA:** **Apply to the Founding 50.**

### Track B — Consumer (design-conscious homeowners)
- **Who:** Taste-driven homeowners who care about craft and longevity, are tired of endless-scroll furniture shopping, and want a designer's eye — whether or not they ever hire one.
- **What they want to hear:** *A designer's judgment, without the intimidation or the price of admission.* Fewer, better choices. Furniture that lasts and ages well. A community shaping something real.
- **Message pillars:**
  1. **A designer's eye, on your terms.** The benefit of a great designer, whether or not you hire one.
  2. **Fewer, righter choices.** A focused selection that fits your room — not a thousand that don't.
  3. **Furniture that earns its place.** Heritage makers, honest materials, built to be handed down.
  4. **Help build it.** The Founding Circle shapes the product and gets named at launch.
- **Surfaces:** home, `/app`, `/founding`, `/makers`, `/materials`, `/journal`.
- **Primary CTA:** **Join the Founding Circle.**

## 3. Voice & tone

Four attributes. Every line should be recognizably at least two of them.

| Attribute | Means | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Confident yet unpretentious** | We know our craft; we don't posture. No superlatives, no hype. | "We show you eight pieces that belong, not eight hundred that might." |
| **Sensory & tangible** | Lead with grain, weight, light, leather — things you can feel. | "Watch morning light catch the grain." |
| **Story-driven** | Provenance and people over specs and SKUs. | "Your factory floor has a story, and your furniture carries it forward." |
| **Midwestern warmth (honest, not hedging)** | Plain-spoken, friendly-neighbor. We'd rather say "not yet" than fake urgency. | "We're not ready to launch yet — but we'd love your help getting there." |

### Tone shifts by moment
- **Trade pages:** more peer-to-peer, more candid about the industry's broken tools. Respect their expertise.
- **Consumer pages:** warmer, more reassuring, more sensory.
- **System/error messages:** briefest possible, human, never blaming the user ("Let's try that again" not "Invalid input").
- **Email from Leah/Kody:** first person, personal, a little unpolished on purpose — build-in-public.

### The test
Read it aloud. If a real person — specifically a warm, smart designer at a kitchen table in Madison — wouldn't say it, rewrite it.

## 4. Lexicon

### Say (house words)
designer-taught · cultivated, not decorated · furniture that belongs · a focused selection · craft over volume · heritage makers · honest materials · built to be handed down · the record of a thing being lived with · running start · the handoff · earned its place · no pay-to-play

### Use carefully
- **"curated" / "curated rooms"** — keep on **SEO/meta surfaces** where it earns search traffic (it's a real query). Minimize in body prose; prefer *designer-chosen, designer-selected, cultivated*. Never stack it ("curated, elevated, bespoke").
- **"AI" / "Aesthete Engine"** — only under the §5 rule. Never as the loud headline.
- **numbers/claims** — only if in §6.

### Don't say (banned)
"AI-powered" (standalone) · "our algorithm optimizes" · "data-driven" · accuracy percentages ("75% → 90%") · "invisible signals" · tap-speed / zoom-behavior / "we notice what you don't realize you're telling us" (reads as surveillance — kills trust with high-end clients and contradicts our own Privacy-First claim) · luxury clichés: elevated, bespoke, curated-overuse, "luxury redefined" · urgency clichés: "limited time," "act now," "don't miss out" · "Coming soon!" with no date · fictional testimonials or fake team members · "unlock exclusive deals" · "based on what others bought"

## 5. The AI narrative (restrained — designers lead)

**The rule:** *Designers lead the sentence. AI is the mechanism, named quietly or not at all.*

- ✅ "Real interior designers teach Patina what belongs in a room like yours."
- ✅ "Designer-taught recommendations — a focused selection, not endless scrolling."
- ❌ "The Aesthete Engine learns your style through invisible signals."
- ❌ "Start at 75% accuracy. Reach 90%+ over time."

**Aesthete Engine** demotes from a trademarked hero to an *optional, quiet explainer* — "a designer-taught recommendation system." It may appear once, low on a page, described in plain terms. It is never the headline, never carries a ™ swagger, never cites accuracy math.

**Why:** our own voice guide says don't sound like a tech startup; high-end trade and clients trust *people and craft*, not model performance; and the surveillance-flavored "we notice what you don't realize you're telling us" language directly contradicts the "Privacy First" promise on the same page. Restraint is the premium move.

### Rewrite reference (the pattern for the whole `/app` pass)
| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "The Aesthete Engine learns your style through invisible signals—then surfaces only the furniture that truly fits." | "Real interior designers teach Patina what belongs in a room like yours — so you see a focused handful of pieces that fit, not a thousand that don't." |
| "Start at 75% accuracy. Reach 90%+ over time." | "The more you explore, the sharper it gets — because designers keep teaching it what 'good' looks like." |
| "Your actions reveal authentic preferences—tap speed, zoom behavior, time spent viewing." | *(cut entirely)* |

## 6. Proof points (the only claims we make)

Use these; don't inflate them. Update this list before making a new claim anywhere.

- **200** Founding Circle spots (cap) · **50** Founding Designers · **15** Founding Makers.
- **Madison, Wisconsin** roots · founded **2023** · founders **Leah & Kody Kochaver**.
- **Middlewest Studio** — Leah's real interior-design practice; the origin and the taste that teaches the system.
- **No sponsored placements, no pay-to-play** — every maker earned placement through craft.
- **Applications read personally** — designers within **7 days**, makers within **2 weeks**; no automated intake.
- Materials credibility (Leah/Kody voice): *white oak (~1,360 Janka), full-grain leather, linen, soapstone — materials that look better the day you hand the piece down than the day it arrived.*
- **Structural integrity warranted 10 years** (from Terms) — "natural wood movement and patina are not defects."

### Honesty rules (non-negotiable)
- The **app and marketplace are pre-launch.** Never imply the app is downloadable today. Say "Founding members get first access when the app launches."
- **Never promise what doesn't exist.** If we say "watch your inbox for a letter from Leah," that letter must actually send (see implementation plan).
- **Date the future or don't mention it.** "We're not ready yet" > "Coming soon!"
- **No fictional people or reviews.** Match cards, quotes, and team members must be real or clearly labeled illustrative.

## 9. System & platform message voice

All success/error/empty/consent strings live in one module (`src/lib/copy/system-messages.ts`) and follow these rules:

- **Errors:** short, human, never blame the user. "That email doesn't look right — mind checking it?" beats "Invalid email." Unify the three "Something went wrong" variants into one warm line: *"Something slipped on our end. Try that again?"*
- **Success:** warm + specific + true. If we say a letter is coming, it's coming. "Welcome to the Founding Circle. Leah's first letter is on its way." (only once the email actually sends).
- **Empty states:** turn absence into anticipation. "The first posts are coming soon — we're writing about the decisions behind building Patina."
- **Consent:** minimal, honest, linked. Keep the one-paragraph banner; add a Privacy Policy link; keep Decline as a real, equal choice.
- **Waiting/pre-launch:** always name the real state. "Not ready yet" with a reason.

## 10. Email voice

- **From a person, not a brand.** Founding + newsletter mail is signed **"Warmly, Kody & Leah."** Personal, first-person, build-in-public.
- **Subject lines:** plain and true, no clickbait. "A first letter from Leah" / "You're in — here's what happens next."
- **Design:** the warm system palette — `#FAF7F2` background, `#2C2926` text, `#E5E2DD` rules — footer `Patina · hello@patina.cloud`. Consistent with the site's paper-warm identity.
- **Every marketing email** carries an unsubscribe/preferences line. Transactional acks may omit it.
- **Never** fake urgency or scarcity in email. The 200-spot cap is real; state it, don't manufacture it.
