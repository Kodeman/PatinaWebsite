1. Madison design and everyday life

First fit: BRAVA’s editorial coverage form. Also suitable for Madison Magazine after its current editorial route is verified. For Isthmus, lead with the working practice and local people, not program benefits.

Selection criterion: A publication serving Madison readers with home, design, local women, or creative-practice coverage. Leah’s work is the reporting substance; the Founding Circle is the timely invitation at the end. BRAVA’s current site separates editorial ideas from paid partner profiles, so submit through its editorial idea form.

Subject: A Madison designer’s eye, and the tools around it

Hello BRAVA team,

I’m Kody Kochaver, co-founder of Patina. I’m sharing a Madison story at the meeting point of home and creative work: Leah Kochaver’s interior-design practice, Middle West Studio, and the furniture platform we’re building together.

We can also explain what we’re learning as we turn that working practice into a product. The public app and marketplace are still pre-launch; the website and founding invitation are live.

The story is here: patina.cloud/about. If it fits your home or local-women coverage, I’d be glad to arrange a conversation with Leah and share the background sheet and image captions.

2. Madison business and origin

First fit: In Business Madison; Wisconsin State Journal’s newsroom. Shorten to the opening, product-state paragraph, and interview offer for City Cast Madison/Madison Minutes.

Selection criterion: A reporter covering how local people turn professional experience into a business. Offer the choices and unresolved questions of building before market launch, with an opportunity to ask both founders questions. A website redesign alone is a weak business-news hook. Selection criterion: A current editorial interest in how designers work, furniture selection, or new approaches to the client/designer relationship. Use this after local outreach, when Kody can support a real trade conversation. It can stand as a Midwest practice story for DESIGN CHICAGO. Dispatch discipline and outreach log

Kody owns the suggested press workflow and maintains the log. Leah joins design-focused interviews and validates studio/project details. Send individually through the listed editorial route. Use the actual recipient’s name only when confirmed on the publication’s current site; a team greeting is preferable to a guessed name.

Use these log fields:

Field Purpose
Outlet, market, official route, last checked Know where the pitch went and when the route was verified
Pitch version and actual angle Identify the story offered, not just the campaign name
Human owner and spokesperson Keep replies and interview requests from going unanswered
Local relevance / reporting evidence Record the reason this outlet is a fit; link any real regional participant’s permission
Planned send, actual send, follow-up due, actual follow-up Separate intention from completed action
Status Prepared / sent / replied / interview requested / declined / no reply / published
Response and next action Capture deadlines, requests, and who will act
Assets offered, credit, permission, exclusivity terms Track what may be reused and whether another project submission conflicts
Published URL and publication date Record actual coverage, not a promised placement
Tagged link, observed referral traffic, observed signups Review outcomes with the launch dashboard; note missing consent or stripped parameters
Preference / do-not-contact Respect the recipient’s instructions

Do not log guessed personal details, add journalists to a marketing list, or record an introduction as a partnership. Do not trade advertising purchases for an assumed editorial result. A reply, an interview, and a published story are separate outcomes.
