# Patina — founder narrative, answers, and interview notes

Prepared 11 September 2026. Editorial drafts grounded in the current website, the founders’ stated direction, and the canonical messaging facts. None of the new first-person language below is a recorded quotation or an approved statement already given to press.

## The story to carry

Lead with two complementary perspectives and one concrete problem: Leah understands what a working designer brings to a room; Kody sees tools that feel detached from that creative work. Patina is the place they are building to bring those things together.

A founder story does not need an invented moment of revelation. There is no sourced story here about a late-night conversation, a frustrating furniture purchase, a shared client, or a single day the idea began. The stronger story is the connection between their actual work and what they are choosing to build.

**Editorial balance:** consumer copy starts with a person and their home; Kody’s copy explains the gap in the tools; Leah’s copy reveals the judgment behind a choice. Makers describe their own documented work when those relationships are ready. The handoff to a designer is a product ambition, always expressed in the pre-launch context.

## STORY-50 — concise introduction / 50 words

Patina brings together Leah Kochaver’s designer’s eye and Kody Kochaver’s instinct for useful tools. Rooted in Madison, they’re building furniture discovery around judgment, craft, and the creative process. The app and marketplace are pre-launch. Their Founding Circle invites people to help shape a more personal way to find what belongs.

## STORY-150 — a personal introduction / 150 words

Patina begins with two ways of seeing. Leah Kochaver understands how a room comes together through her work at Middlewest Studio. Kody Kochaver sees a gap in the tools around creative work: experiences that feel sterile, with too little room for the looking and reconsidering that make design personal.

They’re building Patina in Madison to bring a designer’s judgment within reach. The idea is a way to discover furniture, understand the care behind a piece, and carry your discoveries into a conversation with a designer when you want to go further.

The name reflects their belief in things that gain character through use. A room becomes yours over time. Furniture can become part of its story.

The app and marketplace are pre-launch. The Founding Circle invites people into the building process through studio notes, monthly questions, and first access when the app launches. It is open wherever you call home.

## STORY-400 — the fuller story / 400 words

A room starts to feel like yours through the things you notice. The light at one end of the day. A chair you keep moving closer to the window. A piece that has been with you long enough to carry a story.

Leah Kochaver brings that attention to her work at Middlewest Studio. Her path ran through fine art and communications, then interior design. After twelve years in Seattle, she returned to Wisconsin in 2018 and founded her studio in 2023. Her practice gives Patina its grounding in the decisions a working designer makes: how a room functions, what a material contributes, and what matters to the person living there.

Kody Kochaver comes to the work through technology and product. He has spent two decades making complex systems simpler to use. With Patina, he sees a gap in the tools surrounding creative work. They can feel sterile, disconnected from the experience of looking closely, following an idea, and finding something you could not quite describe beforehand.

Patina brings those perspectives together. Leah leads Design and Experience. Kody leads Technology and Product. From Madison, they are building a more personal way to discover furniture, guided by a designer’s judgment and attentive to the people behind the pieces.

The ambition reaches beyond the first discovery. When someone wants more help, the path should continue into a conversation with a designer, carrying forward what they have noticed about their room and their own preferences. The creative process deserves that continuity.

The name holds a related belief. Patina is the character a surface develops through time and exposure. The founders are drawn to things that grow more meaningful through use, and to a company that can develop with the people around it.

The new website is a first expression of that world. Its Living Study moves from a room to a closer look at materials and making. It invites exploration while being clear about what is ready: the app and marketplace are still in development.

The Founding Circle is a way to take part now. Up to 200 members will receive studio notes, contribute through monthly questions, and get first access when the app launches. Membership is open wherever you call home.

Designers and makers have their own invitations to help shape the work. Together, those perspectives can help Patina become a place where judgment, craft, and personal experience belong in the same conversation.

## Consumer questions

### What can I do with Patina today?

You can explore the website, read the story, and join the Founding Circle. The app and marketplace are still in development. The circle brings you into the building process and gives you first access when the app launches.

### What will I receive after joining?

A first letter from Kody, followed by studio notes and monthly questions that invite your perspective. Founding members get first access when the app launches and keep their founding status. Recognition at launch should reflect whether and how you want your name shared.

### Is this for people who already work with a designer?

It is for people who care about the furniture they live with, whether or not they have worked with a designer. We’re building a way to explore with a designer’s judgment within reach and continue into a fuller design engagement when you want more help.

### Do I need a project underway or a clear design style?

No. The things you like, keep, and find difficult to choose are useful starting points. Monthly questions are an invitation to share your perspective, not a request for a finished design brief.

### Can I buy the furniture shown on the website?

The website is not an open furniture marketplace. Some images are illustrative studies and are labeled that way. Documentary Middle West Studio projects show Leah’s design work; they are not Patina product listings.

### When will the app launch, and what will it cost?

There is no announced public app launch date or product pricing in this launch package. Founding members will hear from us when there is something ready to try. Joining the circle should not be described as a promise of free future services, discounts, or a lifetime paid subscription.

### Is the Founding Circle only for the Midwest?

No. It is open wherever you call home. Patina is rooted in Madison, with early outreach focused on the Chicago–Madison–Minneapolis corridor. That focus is about how we begin, not who can join.

### Does a founding membership guarantee that my suggestion will be built?

Your perspective helps inform the work. Kody and Leah consider it alongside design judgment, maker perspectives, and what can be built well. The monthly notes are an opportunity to explain the decisions, including ideas that need more work.

### What does “200 members” mean?

It is the stated cap for the Founding Circle. It does not tell you how many places have been taken or remain. We do not use an invented remaining-place count.

### Can I stop the emails or keep my name private?

Yes. You can reply to the studio notes to ask us to stop sending them. Public recognition should be a separate choice; tell us if you prefer to remain private. You can also reach Kody and Leah at hello@patina.cloud.

**Consumer destination:** [Join the Founding Circle](https://patina.cloud/founding).

## Designer questions

### What is the Founding 50?

An invitation for 50 working interior designers to help shape Patina’s tools, the language used to describe furniture, and the future path from personal discovery to a design engagement. The public app and marketplace remain pre-launch.

### What would I contribute?

Your experience of real design work: what sourcing tools miss, what descriptions are useful, and what you would want to know about a client before a conversation. The published program includes monthly conversations with Kody and Leah and a cohort of peers.

### What is the cost or time commitment?

The published Founding 50 invitation states that there is no fee to join. It describes monthly conversations but does not specify an hourly commitment. Ask Kody and Leah about participation expectations before deciding whether it fits your practice.

### Does applying mean I have been accepted?

No. The automatic email confirms that your application arrived. Kody reviews it with Leah. They respond within seven days with an invitation to talk or an update on timing.

### Will joining bring me clients immediately?

The public consumer app is pre-launch. Founding designers are intended to be the first designers people can connect with when that app launches. The invitation does not guarantee a number of leads, projects, or revenue.

### Is Patina trying to replace a designer’s judgment?

Working designers provide the judgment the experience is being built around. The aim is to make that perspective more accessible and create a thoughtful path to a fuller designer engagement. Technology supports that work.

### Who owns the work, feedback, or material I share?

The marketing invitation does not define intellectual-property or licensing terms. Ask Kody and Leah for the applicable terms before sharing confidential client material or work for reuse. You can describe a workflow problem without submitting private project information.

**Designer destination:** [Apply to the Founding 50](https://patina.cloud/designers#apply).

## Maker questions

### What is the founding maker invitation?

Patina is inviting 15 founding makers to help shape how furniture and the care behind it are discovered and understood. The app and marketplace are pre-launch. An application introduces your workshop and begins a conversation about fit.

### What should I include?

Your workshop name and location, contact details, materials and process, and a website or portfolio where Kody and Leah can see the work. If you have a trade program, explain how it operates today. A specific piece can help illustrate the decisions behind your approach.

### How is placement considered?

The work and its fit with Patina matter. There are no sponsored placements or pay-to-play slots. The team looks for considered design, material quality, and care in the craft.

### Are the makers pictured on the website already partners?

An illustrative workshop image does not identify an accepted partner. Documentary imagery should identify its real subject and credit. The current launch package does not establish a named roster of accepted maker partners.

### Does an application commit my workshop to a commercial agreement?

The application begins an introduction and review. It does not in itself establish acceptance, exclusivity, order volume, or a commercial agreement. Discuss partnership terms directly with Kody and Leah before committing the workshop.

### What commission or delivery terms apply?

This package does not set commission, fulfillment, returns, territory, or exclusivity terms. The no-pay-to-play principle concerns placement; it should not be stretched into an invented claim that every future commercial service is free.

### When will I hear back?

Kody reviews applications with Leah and responds within two weeks with next steps or an update on timing. The immediate acknowledgement confirms receipt.

**Maker destination:** [Apply to Partner](https://patina.cloud/makers/apply).

## Proposed interview answers

These are rehearsal answers for the founders to make their own. They are **draft first-person language**, not quotations available for an attributed press release. The press package may use the grounded third-person facts without converting these paragraphs into speech already given.

### Kody — Why are you building Patina?

I keep seeing a gap between creative work and the tools around it. Design involves looking closely, following an idea, reconsidering it. The tools can feel sterile beside all of that. I want Patina to feel like it belongs in the process. Leah brings the judgment of a working designer, and I’m building the technology around that perspective and the people who will use it.

### Leah — What does your practice bring to the idea?

My work at Middlewest Studio starts with understanding how someone lives and what they are drawn to. There are practical questions about a room, but also personal ones. I want that kind of attention in Patina. A designer’s judgment helps connect a piece to a person and a room. That is the part I want people to have more access to.

### Kody — Why does the website feel like a studio?

The website is our first chance to make the idea tangible. We wanted someone to move through a room, look closer at the materials, and feel the care that goes into making. It reflects how we want the tools to feel. It also gives us a place to be honest about the stage we’re at: the website is open, and the app and marketplace are still being built.

### Leah — What are you looking for from founding members?

Their own point of view. What they keep, what they struggle to find, what makes a room feel like home. You don’t have to know a set of design terms to contribute. We want those observations close to the work, and we want to explain how they help us think through decisions.

### Kody — What is different about your approach?

We begin with a designer’s judgment and the care behind the furniture. We’re building toward a focused discovery experience and a path to working with a designer when someone wants to go further. The point is to preserve what a person has learned about their own preferences along the way. We’re still building it, so that is the direction we can speak to today.

### Leah — Why begin in Madison and the Midwest?

Madison is home to the work we’re doing, and my design practice gives us relationships to begin with. We’re starting those conversations close to home, then reaching toward Chicago and Minneapolis. The Founding Circle is open beyond the region. The roots give the story a place to begin.

### Kody — Is this an AI company?

We’re building a furniture platform around the judgment of working designers. Technology is part of how that judgment can become more accessible. What matters to us is the experience: finding something that belongs in your room and having a path to a designer when you need more help.

### Kody — What is available now?

The redesigned website is live, and people can join the Founding Circle or apply through the designer and maker invitations. The app and marketplace are pre-launch. We have not announced a public app launch date. Founding members will have first access when it launches.

### Kody — How do you know this will work?

We’re early, and we need to learn from the people we’re building for. Leah’s practice and my experience with tools give us a point of view. The Founding Circle and the designer and maker invitations let us bring more perspectives into the work. We should be able to explain what we’ve learned and what we’ve changed as we go.

## Responses when the answer is not settled

Use a plain answer rather than a speculative promise.

- **Funding, revenue, growth, or enrollment totals:** “We’re not sharing a verified figure in this launch package.” Give a figure only after Kody has confirmed its meaning and reporting date.
- **App launch date or event:** “The app and marketplace are pre-launch. We haven’t announced a public app launch date or a launch event.”
- **Named partners:** “We’re inviting designers and makers to help shape Patina. We’ll introduce specific relationships when they are ready to be shared.”
- **Pricing or detailed commercial terms:** “That isn’t specified in this launch package. We can discuss the relevant terms directly before anyone makes a commitment.”
- **Product results or time saved:** “We’re still building and learning. We aren’t making a performance claim.”
- **Illustrative photography:** “Those images are illustrative studies, labeled on the website. They show the direction of the experience and do not document an available product or a maker partnership.”

## Editorial use

Use the 50-word story for introductions and small placements; the 150-word story for partner emails and background notes; the 400-word story for the launch book or a founder feature. Keep the press boilerplate synchronized with the facts, while letting the longer story do the emotional work. Do not add an origin anecdote simply to make the story sound more dramatic.

Founder review should focus on whether the language sounds like them and accurately describes their motivation. Facts already sourced here do not need a new round of discovery. Any new personal incident, direct quotation, partnership, client example, or product claim needs its own evidence before inclusion.
