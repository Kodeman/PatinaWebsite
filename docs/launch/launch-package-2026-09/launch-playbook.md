# Patina — the Living Study launch

Prepared 11 September 2026 · Campaign plan and working assets · Nothing scheduled or distributed

## The decision

Introduce Patina through people who can explain why it should exist. Begin with Leah’s client relationships, invite people into the Founding Circle, and use their questions to sharpen the public introduction. Follow with selective Madison press, then relevant conversations across Chicago, Madison, and Minneapolis. The website is the place to explore; joining is the first meaningful action.

The redesigned website has been deployed. The public app and marketplace remain pre-launch. This campaign opens a conversation about what is being built; it does not announce a downloadable app, shopping availability, or a roster of accepted partners.

**Campaign headline:** A place at the beginning.

**Brand signature:** Where Time Adds Value.

**Core invitation:** We’re building a more personal way to discover furniture, with a designer’s judgment, a maker’s care, and your own point of view. Join the Founding Circle and help shape what comes next.

“A place at the beginning” is the campaign invitation already used on the founding page, not a replacement tagline. Retain the Patina Strata in every branded composition.

## What each audience should understand

Public campaign emphasis: ten consumer/founder posts and two dedicated trade posts in the twelve-post library. This is an editorial choice, not a media-spend allocation. Designers and makers receive their own thoughtful correspondence; the homepage invitation remains consumer-led.

The 200-member Founding Circle, 50 founding designers, and 15 founding makers describe program sizes. They are neither launch goals nor claims about remaining places. Membership is open beyond the Midwest. The regional focus describes outreach, not future service coverage.

## The story that holds the campaign together

Leah brings the judgment of a working interior designer through Middle West Studio. Kody sees a gap in tools that feel sterile and detached from the creative process. Patina brings those perspectives together: furniture discovery that belongs within the work of making a home.

Let each voice carry its own subject. Leah writes about what she notices in rooms, materials, and the way people live. Kody writes about the choices involved in building the tools. Shared brand copy gives the reader a clear next step. Avoid repeating the entire origin story in every post.

The campaign unfolds in three chapters:

1. **Come into the study.** Explain the point of view and open the invitation. Lead with the room and the feeling of exploring the website.
2. **Look at the decisions.** Share one design observation, one product question, and the supporting designer and maker invitations. A small, real decision is more useful than a progress montage.
3. **Help shape what stays.** Ask the first monthly question, listen, and return with what was learned. When no decision has been made, say that plainly.

The full voice treatments and reader questions are in [the founder narrative and FAQ](communications/founder-narrative-and-faq.md).

## A workload two founders can sustain

Proposed weekly operating allowance: **Leah, about 2 hours; Kody, about 4 hours**, plus a one-time joint 90-minute package review. These are planning assumptions, not commitments already made by either founder.

- Leah: select relationships, personalize invitations, review design language and client context, read design-related replies.
- Kody: manage the campaign links and publishing, send product/trade correspondence, answer replies, coordinate press, review submissions with Leah.
- Together: a 30-minute weekly review of new members, application response times, repeated questions, and next week’s work.

Protect the published review windows: designer applications within 7 days and maker applications within 2 weeks. Check application queues on working days and once before a weekend if a deadline would otherwise slip. Pause new outreach when response capacity is strained. Automatic acknowledgements confirm receipt; personal review still needs time.

**Base plan: $0 in incremental media spend.** Use the existing website, social accounts, email, and these reusable assets. Existing service subscriptions and founder time still have costs. No paid distribution, event, agency retainer, or photography purchase is booked by this package.

Do not buy broad launch traffic to compensate for an unclear invitation. If the first two weeks establish that people understand the offer and the joining experience works, a later paid test can be scoped around one audience, one creative, and a capped budget. That test needs its own budget decision; it is not necessary to execute this plan.

## Objectives and decision rules

These are **proposed six-week planning targets, not forecasts or industry benchmarks**. The existing analytics baseline is too small to support a reliable acquisition forecast. Agree or revise the targets at T0, then rebase after Week 2 using the actual reachable audience.

| Outcome | Working target | How to judge it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founding Circle growth | 25 new, non-test enrollments | Reconcile persisted new records; do not sum browser and server events or assume every repeat submission is a new member. |
| Designer interest | 4 applications worth a personal conversation | Record applications received separately from reviewed/accepted applicants. |
| Maker interest | 2 applications worth a personal conversation | Assess actual fit and readiness to tell a documented craft story. |
| Member participation | 5 substantive replies to founder notes/questions | A reply that helps understand a room, a choice, or a difficulty; no minimum word count. |
| Editorial interest | 1 relevant journalist conversation | Coverage is an earned outcome; neither an email send nor a paid placement is coverage. |
| Delivery discipline | 12 prepared organic posts; up to 20 appropriate client invitations, 6 designer and 4 maker introductions over six weeks | Reduce volume when the available audience is smaller or response quality demands time. |

Keep decision rules simple:

- If three independent people misunderstand the same benefit, rewrite that explanation before broadening distribution.
- If a form or reply path fails, fix it before the next send. A functioning website takes precedence over the calendar.
- If a channel has only a handful of observable visitors, report counts and questions; do not crown a conversion-rate winner.
- If people engage but do not join, ask what they expected from membership before changing the visual campaign.
- If replies concentrate on a specific room or difficulty, use that theme for the next founder note, without attributing an individual’s story without permission.
